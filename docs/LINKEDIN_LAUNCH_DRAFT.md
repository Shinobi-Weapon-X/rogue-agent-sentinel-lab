# LinkedIn launch draft — for editing after independent testing

🛡️ When an AI model recommends an action, we can question its reasoning. But when an AI agent is empowered to act, the governance question changes. Who decides what the agent is permitted to do, and who can stop it?

🔍 This question inspired my third weekend portfolio experiment, the Rogue Agent Sentinel Lab. Using three fictional financial crime agents, the project examines proposed tool calls, bounded authority, sensitive approval checkpoints and simulated human intervention.

📊 The results are deliberately revealing. A deterministic policy gate caught all eight scripted unsafe proposals in the fictional holdout. Yet the supplemental anomaly detector flagged only two and produced 16 false alarms. Because the scenarios mirror the permissions, the perfect policy score is not proof of real security. The broader lesson is that statistical monitoring cannot substitute for independent enforcement.

🖥️ Visitors can explore the Agent Registry, Activity Monitor, Rogue Detection, Policy Enforcement, Human Review and AI Governance sections, with explanations, local suspension simulations and downloadable fictional case records.

💭 To me, governing agentic AI means moving beyond confidence scores toward enforceable boundaries, trusted human authority, meaningful challenge and evidence that can withstand scrutiny.

🌱 I am using these small practical exercises to develop my understanding of AI engineering and governance, while being explicit about what has not been demonstrated. The project simulates proposals only. It does not monitor or stop real agents.
